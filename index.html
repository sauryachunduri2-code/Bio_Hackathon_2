The 3-Beat Story

  1. 1. The Gap: "Genetic repositories are heavily skewed toward European ancestry. The UK Biobank, one of the largest public datasets, is 94% European."
  2. 2. The Harm: "The same risk-prediction tool can be up to 4.5× less accurate for a patient of African ancestry, so the people it was never built on get the worst care." (Martin et al., Nature Genetics 2019)
  3. 3. The Fix: "When the data matches the patient, accuracy jumps. Scores built on BioBank Japan were almost 50% more accurate for East Asians than UK Biobank scores. Our explorer maps biobank blindspots so they can be fixed."

    Evidence base

    Scientific Literature & Citations

    Key clinical case studies and research supporting genomic equity auditing.

    Source: Martin et al. (2019) — Clinical Disparities in Polygenic Risk Scores

    Nature Genetics, "Clinical use of current polygenic risk scores may exacerbate health disparities"

    • Compared with European ancestry, prediction accuracy from UK Biobank data was 4.5× lower for African ancestry, 2.0× lower for East Asians, and 1.6× lower for both Hispanic/Latino Americans and South Asians.
    • Primary driver: dataset imbalance. The UK Biobank (almost half a million people) draws 94% of its data from participants of European ancestry.
    • Proof of fix: scores built from the BioBank Japan dataset were almost 50% more accurate for East Asians than scores from UK Biobank data, so ancestry-matched data gives more precise risk predictions.
    • Call to action: recruit more minority participants and make the data open, or healthcare inequities will widen (Alicia Martin, PhD).
